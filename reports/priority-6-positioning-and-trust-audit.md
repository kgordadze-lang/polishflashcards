### The core finding

**Repository finding.** The strongest statement of the locked positioning anywhere on the site is the lede of `/guide/` — a secondary page:

> Built by a foreigner living in Poland and learning the language through everyday life - with practical flashcards, clear explanations, pronunciation audio, and free interactive practice for every topic.
> — [guide/index.html:126](guide/index.html:126), generated by [build_pages.py](build_pages.py)

The homepage hero says only:

> **Learn the Polish you'll *actually* use.**
> Polish flashcards with Polish pronunciation audio - everyday vocabulary, useful grammar, conversation practice, and phrases from real podcasts.
> — [index.html:1077-1079](index.html:1077)

The hero is a good *product* line and a weak *positioning* line. "The Polish you'll actually use" is true of a learner in Chicago as much as one in Kraków. Nothing tells an arriving visitor that this was built by someone in their situation, for their situation.

**Supported inference.** A visitor arriving from a search like "Polish phrases for the doctor" sees a competent flashcard app but no signal that it was built for life in Poland specifically — which is precisely the differentiator the Phase 0 brief locks.

### The trust gap that matters most

**Repository finding.** The repository contains genuinely impressive quality machinery that is invisible to users:

- `validate_content.py` — a 150 KB deterministic content validator
- a frozen forward baseline covering 1,675 ids, 13,387 wording entries, 1,312 policy entries, 1,777 structure entries (`reports/priority-5-forward-frozen-baseline.json`)
- 149 Python tests and 32 JavaScript suites (9,535 assertions), all passing this session
- `verify_audio.py` proving 3,377/3,377 audio coverage with zero orphans

None of this is communicated publicly. Instead, the only public quality signal is the sentence describing informal help from Polish speakers the founder knows ([index.html:1627](index.html:1627)). That sentence is not shown to be false and names no particular person, but its **scope and coverage are undocumented**, and it is **open to a broader reading than intended** — a visitor may take it to mean a Polish teacher systematically checks the app.

**This is the central trust recommendation of Phase 0: replace a relationship-based signal with a process signal.** A process statement is clearer, does not depend on any individual, and stays accurate as the content grows. The approved wording already exists:

> "New major content releases follow a structured linguistic and audio-review process."

## 5. Recommended internal hierarchy

**Requires human copy approval. Not implemented. Wording is illustrative, not final.**

The constraint from the Phase 0 brief is decisive: the learning interface and learning controls must remain central, and the homepage must not become a marketing page. The recommendation below therefore *replaces text already present* rather than adding sections, and the topic list stays immediately below the fold-line it occupies today.

### 1. One-line promise (replaces the current H1)

> **Practical Polish for real life in Poland.**

Retaining the existing line as a secondary option is also defensible; the trade-off is that "the Polish you'll actually use" tests better as a *product* line while the proposed line carries the locked positioning. A hybrid worth considering:

> **Learn the Polish you'll actually use — for real life in Poland.**

### 2. Supporting statement (replaces the current hero sub)

> Everyday vocabulary, grammar, conversations, and Polish pronunciation audio for adults at A1–B1 who live in Poland, are moving here, or need Polish for daily life.

This is the only place the A1–B1 level and the Poland context need to appear; naming them once is enough.

### 3. Founder statement (one line, home; expanded on About)

> Built by a foreigner living in Poland, for learners who want more than textbook Polish.

### 4. Proof points (extend the badge row from 2 to 5, or 3 + 2)

> Genuinely free · No account required · Pronunciation audio · Interactive practice · Works offline

If five badges crowd the mobile hero, the recommended split is three badges (Genuinely free · No account required · Works offline) with pronunciation audio and interactive practice carried by the supporting statement. **Requires live-browser verification** for layout at 320 px.

### 5. First learning call to action

**No change recommended.** The topic list already *is* the call to action, it already renders directly beneath the hero, and the app already opens on Vocabulary → A1 statelessly every time ([index.html:2338-2340](index.html:2338)). This is the correct design and Priority 6 should protect it, not replace it with a marketing CTA.

The one addition worth considering is a single short line above the topic list for first-time visitors only — see the first-visit activation audit, which recommends against anything heavier.

## 6. Quality explanation — recommended placement

**Requires human copy approval.**

Recommended: a short "How the content is looked after" block on **About**, not on the homepage.

> New major content releases follow a structured linguistic and audio-review process. Content is checked automatically before every release, and pronunciation audio is verified against the full phrase library.

This references the four permitted concepts (structured linguistic review, pronunciation-audio review, deterministic content safeguards, automated testing, review before major releases) without exposing implementation detail. It must **not** claim that all historical content was professionally reviewed, that every card received identical native-speaker review, or that any named person validates the application.

---

## 7. "Why it is free" — recommended

**Requires human copy approval.** Currently absent everywhere. A single sentence on About would close a real trust gap, because "genuinely free" invites the question "what's the catch?" and the honest answer is unusually good here: no ads, no analytics, no account, no data collection. Suggested shape:

> Po polsku is free because it costs me almost nothing to run: there are no accounts, no servers holding your data, no advertising, and no analytics. It started as my own study tool and it stays free.

**Assumption requiring validation:** that the founder is comfortable stating running costs in these terms.
